A Rockefeller Foundation × Gallup collaboration — response to briefing, July 2026

The AI Worker Pulse

A fast, credible, recurring national read on how artificial intelligence is actually landing on American workers — their security, agency, mobility, and capacity to make the transition. The trusted signal that does not yet exist, fielded on the largest probability-based panel in the country and built to be quoted, tracked, and acted on.

The opportunity

There is no trusted, recurring national indicator that tracks how AI is landing on American workers in real time. The country has strong data on adoption — how many people use AI, in which tools, how often. What it lacks is a consistent read on the worker side of the transition: whether people feel secure, whether they have a say, whether they can retrain and move, and where the disruption is actually concentrated.

The moment is not abstract. In Gallup's latest workforce data, more than half of U.S. employees now use AI at work — the first time the measure has crossed 50% — even as the share of workers who believe AI is unlikely to eliminate their job keeps falling. Adoption and unease are rising together, and no one is measuring, release over release, who is bearing the cost of the transition and who is capturing its gains.

The Rockefeller Foundation and Gallup can close that gap this fall. The AI Worker Pulse is designed as a fast first signal in 2026 — and a repeatable instrument that can scale into the durable public index policymakers monitor, employers benchmark against, and media cite. It gives the worker experience of AI the same standing that adoption metrics already have.

Adoption & macro data

The headline product

At Risk · Navigating · Advancing

Illustrative structure — not findings
24%At Risk
51%Navigating
25%Advancing

At Risk

Bearing the brunt

Facing active displacement pressure, wage erosion, or job loss — with limited capacity to adapt.

Navigating

In active transition

Stable today but uncertain about tomorrow; accessing some retraining or shifting roles, outcomes still unclear. Likely the largest tier — and the most consequential for policy.

Advancing

Gaining ground

Using AI as an amplifier of skills, earnings, and trajectory, with high agency and strong mobility.

One simple, repeatable lens — “In Q4 2026, X% of American workers are At Risk, Y% Navigating, Z% Advancing” — trackable over time and cut by sector, geography, age, education, and race. It is the quotable product that carries every release, built the same way Gallup's American Job Quality Study sorts workers across five dimensions of job quality.

The instrument

Built on validated items — extended to fill the gap

The Pulse does not start from a blank page. Gallup already fields a quarterly Workforce study with tested items on AI at work. The Pulse carries those forward for continuity and comparability, then adds the worker-transition items no recurring national instrument captures. Final wording is set jointly in the design sprint.

Items that fill the gap

The worker-transition measures that place each respondent into a tier.

Re-employment confidence
Confidence in finding equivalent work within six months if AI eliminated the role tomorrow.
Voice in AI rollout
Whether the worker has a say in how AI tools are introduced into their job.
Skill-path clarity
Whether the worker knows what new skills a move to a different role would require.
Retrain vs. reduce
When an employer automates, whether they retrain and redeploy or cut the role.
Active preparation
Whether the worker is training, learning, or exploring new roles right now.
Upward mobility via AI
Whether AI has opened a promotion, new role, or higher pay they wouldn't otherwise have had.
Geographic placement
Urban, rural, or heartland classification — the lens federal and academic data lack.

The result: a Pulse that is comparable to Gallup's existing tracking from day one, yet measures the transition experience nothing else captures — and rolls up into the At Risk / Navigating / Advancing tiers.

Why Gallup

The workplace is Gallup's home ground

No organization has studied work more deeply, or for longer, than Gallup. Its State of the Global Workplace is the world's definitive read on the employee experience, drawing on the Gallup World Poll across roughly 160 countries and more than 2.6 million employed respondents over its trend. Its Q12 is the most tested and validated employee-engagement instrument in existence, and CliftonStrengths has been completed by over 30 million people.

That home-ground advantage is why the Pulse is not a cold start. Gallup already fields a quarterly Workforce study of more than 20,000 U.S. workers with validated items tracking AI use, comfort, manager support, and integration — the exact infrastructure the Pulse extends. It means Gallup can confirm, before a new item is written, precisely where the Pulse fills a genuine gap rather than duplicating what already runs.

A standalone survey on the Gallup Panel — the largest probability-based panel in the United States — is the recommended vehicle for the first fielding: fast enough to release this fall, deep enough to support the segmentation, and rigorous enough to be cited without argument.

How we work together

Answers to the Foundation's questions

The open questions from the briefing, addressed directly. Select any to expand.

01What partnership model do you recommend?

A direct contract model: the Foundation engages Gallup for the work. Gallup's public-facing programs are typically three to five years in nature, but this can start smaller — the 2026 Pulse is a natural first commitment that proves the instrument before any decision to scale.

02What fielding vehicle, and what cadence?

For the first read, Gallup recommends a standalone survey on the Gallup Panel — nationally representative, with efficiency built in. For a recurring series, the recommendation is biannual deeper waves using the Gallup Pulse vehicle, with the option to add lighter quarterly tracking items to Gallup's existing Workforce study between waves to keep the signal alive.

03Is a fall 2026 release achievable?

Yes. A September fielding with an October release is feasible if work starts immediately; a November–December release is preferred for a stronger, better-amplified debut. Holding the timeline requires an early agreement, prompt sign-off on the instrument, and alignment on the release plan.

04What sample sizes support the cuts you need?

Gallup offers three scenarios on the Gallup Panel — n=3,000, n=5,000, and n=10,000 — scaling the granularity of the cuts. A national read sequences up toward sector, demographic, and the urban / rural / heartland geography as sample grows. The interactive builder below lets you see how each choice moves scope and cost.

05Is the expertise in-house, or do you bring in partners?

The work is done in-house, end-to-end — instrument design, sampling, fielding, analysis, and communications — using Gallup's existing sampling frames and its in-house strategic communications team. Workplace scientists lead instrument design without requiring an outside academic partner. An optional technical advisory board of Gallup senior scientists and external experts can pressure-test the instrument and act as public champions.

06How does communications work on joint releases?

Gallup's strategic communications team engages at the front end, shaping a research agenda that fits the Foundation's goals, then pitches to its established media bench and works alongside the Foundation's team and any grantees or PR partners. Gallup develops the assets — data stories, video, static social content — amplified through official Gallup channels. The Foundation leads narrative and policy framing; Gallup ensures findings are structured for maximum pickup.

07How is branding and byline handled?

The Foundation's call. Releases can be co-branded Rockefeller × Gallup, anchored fully by the Rockefeller brand, or Gallup-led — the Gallup brand is available and tends to lift pickup in the national domain, but it is not required. On publications, Rockefeller leaders provide a foreword and review and approve, while Gallup does the bulk of the writing — a collaborative model with the Foundation guiding and Gallup creating.

08Who owns the data?

The Foundation owns the data for any new items created through the partnership, and owns the resulting data product. Gallup retains IP for its existing, validated items. Terms on public release of toplines or microdata and on third-party secondary analysis are set together in scoping.

09How will we know it's working?

Gallup tracks uptake against goals — policymaker use, academic citations, and media mentions — and the strategic communications team builds a plan to get the report cited in the publications of interest. A further marker is presence at major convenings such as the Aspen Ideas Festival and the World Economic Forum. Together these signals show whether the Pulse is on its way to becoming a lasting national reference.
