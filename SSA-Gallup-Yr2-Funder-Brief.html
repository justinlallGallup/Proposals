The Program
Measuring the True Scope of Scams in America

The Stop Scams Alliance–Gallup initiative is an ongoing research effort to uncover the real prevalence and impact of scams in the United States. For years, the country’s understanding of the problem has been distorted by how it was measured — leaving Americans and policymakers with a picture that badly understated the scale of the issue.

Four challenges, in particular, have skewed the national understanding of scams:

  • Many measurements combine scams and other types of fraud into a single metric, blurring what is actually happening.
  • Small sample sizes have not allowed scam experience to be broken out by scam type.
  • Some measurements rely on opt-in samples that are not representative of the country — systematically undercounting elderly and lower-income Americans.
  • Official government statistics are based on victim reports, and therefore miss the experiences of the majority of U.S. scam victims.
What Year One Revealed
The First Nationally Representative View of Scams
Launched in 2025, the program produced the most comprehensive view of scams ever developed from a probability-based, truly nationally representative survey.

The research also examined why so much goes unreported: many victims do not believe reporting will help them recover their money or prevent future scams, and others simply do not know how to report. And it explored the emotional and mental toll of scams on victims and their families — a dimension official statistics never capture.

Year Two
From Baseline to Trend

What we will do in Year Two

For Year Two, we will establish ongoing tracking of core measures from the Year 1 survey to assess whether the U.S. is making progress in the battle with scams. We will increase the sample from 5,000 to 10,000 U.S. adults — enabling far better reporting by scam type and scam entry point, so leaders can understand the full life cycle of a scam and disaggregate its impact by type.

We will strategically oversample to unlock reportability by new cohorts, such as Gen Z scam victims, and retain a core module from Year 1 while developing a new module to explore emerging topics. As in Year 1, we will conduct in-depth qualitative interviews with scam victims following the survey to add the human context the numbers alone cannot convey.

We will produce a comprehensive public report, publicize the findings through major convenings, and host a launch event at Gallup’s Washington, D.C. headquarters.

Scope of Work
What Year Two Delivers
  • An updated Gallup survey instrumentDesigned to explore the nature of scams, how victims are targeted, and how experiences differ across demographic groups. It builds on the Year 1 survey and retains key items for year-over-year trending, while developing new items through a design process that centers input from telecommunications, financial-institution, and advocacy experts. It also includes in-depth qualitative interviews with fraud victims to complement the quantitative data.
  • A representative survey of 10,000 U.S. adultsFielded through Gallup’s proprietary, probability-based Gallup Panel.
  • A comprehensive public report (~30 pages)Summarizing the findings with graphics, narrative insights, qualitative accounts, and a detailed methodology statement.
  • De-identified data for partnersDelivered to enable further internal analysis by partner organizations.
  • An updated digital research hubOn Gallup.com, to host the final report.
  • A study launch event at the Gallup Building, Washington, D.C.Convening philanthropists, policy implementers, and influencers to discuss findings, explore actionable solutions, and identify areas for future research. Gallup and Stop Scams Alliance will jointly invite speakers and attendees.
  • A Gallup News article & media outreachHighlighting the research, with targeted media pitching from Gallup’s strategic communications team to ensure the study garners significant attention.

The Partnership
What Partnership with Stop Scams Alliance Offers

Stop Scams Alliance brings together a cross-industry consortium of technology companies, telecom, and financial institutions to collaborate on how to measure scams — a complex undertaking that requires multidisciplinary expertise. This multi-sector approach delivers better analysis and greater credibility for the final report.
