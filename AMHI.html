The American Men’s Health Index

Built by Gallup, carried by Antenna Up, and designed to become the number the country measures against for the next twenty years. This summary sets out the opportunity to launch it.

In Brief

American men are losing ground — and the national conversation about it runs far ahead of the evidence. There is no trusted, shared measure of how men are actually doing, or whether it is getting better or worse. Gallup and Antenna Up propose to build one: the American Men’s Health Index, an annual national benchmark released each September, and the foundation of a future center for research on American men. We are seeking founding funders to launch it — a $5M commitment over its first years, with $1M to begin the work.

The Need

A national issue without a number

By nearly every measure, American men and boys are falling behind — in education, in the workforce, in health and in connection. They die younger, account for the large majority of deaths of despair, and report a steep decline in friendship and community. None of this stands against anyone else’s progress, but men’s declining health carries direct consequences for workforce strength, public health, national security and the health of American civil society. The wellbeing of American men is inseparable from the wellbeing of American women, families and communities.

The issue has become a genuine national concern. Yet for all the attention, the field has no shared, credible fact base. The research that exists is scattered and inconsistent, and into that gap step the loudest voices rather than the most trusted ones. There is no single number the country can point to — no way to say, with authority, whether the state of American men is improving or declining.

The Solution

The American Men’s Health Index

One credible number, released every year, that gives the country a clear and trusted answer — and a benchmark to rally around for the next twenty years.

The American Men’s Health Index is a single, authoritative annual measure of the state of American men — across physical, mental and spiritual health, with spiritual understood as purpose and belonging. Built entirely on original Gallup research, it captures how American men are doing and moves year over year.

It gives the country something it has never had: a clear, trusted answer to whether men’s health is improving or declining, and a benchmark that can hold governments, employers and communities to account. Released each September and fully documented, it is designed to become the figure the press, employers and policymakers cite as a matter of course.

How We Build It

What creating the Index takes

The Index is the product of a deliberate, rigorous process — original national research, informed by the field’s leading thinkers, and carried to the country through a landmark report and a coordinated campaign.

We start with the definitive questions. Working with leading researchers, clinicians and thinkers on the state of American men, we design a study that measures men’s experiences across the domains that matter — health, work, family, purpose and belonging, and civic life — so the Index rests on the best current understanding of the issue, not a single point of view.

We field it on the Gallup Panel. The study is conducted as a nationally representative survey of American men on the Gallup Panel — the largest probability-based panel in the United States — producing original data no existing source captures, with one clear, documented methodology behind every number.

We turn it into a landmark report and a national moment. Gallup authors the inaugural report and constructs the Index; Antenna Up carries it into the culture through its summits, its community and trusted voices, and a coordinated amplification campaign — so the number is not just published, but heard by the leaders, employers and policymakers positioned to act on it.

The Platform & Public Voice

Antenna Up

A cultural platform dedicated to the health and wellbeing of American men and boys — and, in barely a year, a proven one. It has convened hundreds of leaders, businesses and brands around two national summits: the Marketing to Men Summit, on the fuller representation of men in media and advertising, and Healthier Men at Work, connecting male worker health to measurable business outcomes. Its sponsors, speakers and influencers have reported immediate relationship value with no negative backlash — evidence that serious institutions and influential voices will engage when the numbers are credible and the tone is constructive.

It carries the Index into the culture through those convenings, a growing national community, and standing relationships across the media and influencers who shape the conversation. At its head is spokesperson Janna Meyrowitz Turner — a communications strategist with two decades building coalitions and shaping public narratives, who founded and led a nationally recognized agency spanning consumer brands, culture and sport, and brings a credible history in American women’s empowerment to this work.
