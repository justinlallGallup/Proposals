The Work So Far
A body of evidence the field now runs on

The Gallup and Workhuman partnership did something the recognition category had never had: it put rigorous, nationally representative measurement behind an idea leaders felt but could not prove. Beginning with Unleashing the Human Element at Work: Transforming Workplaces Through Recognition, the research established recognition as a strategic driver of engagement, culture, wellbeing and retention — and gave leaders a language for it. The reports that followed carried the work into wellbeing, equity and belonging, business impact, and the longitudinal evidence linking high-quality recognition to who stays and who leaves.

That work was widely read, widely cited, and widely acted on. There is no need to re-litigate it here — the case is made, and the numbers below are a reminder rather than an argument.

The foundational study surveyed more than 7,600 U.S. employees, with companion measurement in the U.K. and Ireland, and introduced the framework the field still uses: the five pillars of strategic recognition.

Later waves tracked nearly 3,500 employees longitudinally and drew on surveys of 10,000+ working adults across the wellbeing, equity and retention reports.

Where It Goes Next
The questions the next wave can answer
The original work proved recognition matters. The frontier now is what recognition should become — as the workforce, its tools and its leaders all change at once. A handful of directions worth exploring together.

New frameworks for recognition

The five pillars set the foundation. The next wave can evolve the model — sharpening how recognition quality is defined and measured, and building the successor framework that captures what high-impact recognition looks like in the workplace taking shape now.

Recognition and the leader of the future

Recognition is becoming a defining leadership capability, not a program to delegate. The research can position it as an imperative for the leader of the future — quantifying how recognition behavior separates the managers who hold teams together from those who lose them.

Recognition in the AI-powered workforce

As AI reshapes how work gets done, it reshapes what people need to feel seen. A dedicated module can measure how the AI-powered workforce experiences recognition — whether it deepens human connection or dilutes it, and what leaders must do to keep recognition human as the tools change.

Quality — and the link to values

Beyond whether recognition happens lies how good it is, and whether it reinforces what the organization says it stands for. The next wave can add nuance on recognition quality and connect recognition directly to an organization’s stated values — the mechanism that turns a moment of thanks into culture.

01

Survey Instrument

The instrument is where the study is designed, and Gallup builds it deliberately. It starts with a structured review of existing materials — Workhuman’s most recent recognition research alongside new Gallup workplace and recognition data — so the next study builds on everything the partnership and the wider field have already established. That review is paired with stakeholder interviews with Workhuman leaders to surface the questions this next chapter most needs to answer. Gallup then drafts the questionnaire and puts it through cognitive testing — one-on-one interviews confirming every item is understood as intended and travels cleanly across markets — before it is finalized for field.

International Markets

Extend the study across EMEA. Eleven markets are on by default to mirror the established survey; add or remove any market and set its sample. Each market is fielded on the same instrument so measurement is consistent across every country. Non-English markets include full translation and localization of the instrument, priced into each market below.

Markets default to n=500 to mirror the established survey. We recommend n=1,000 per market, which enables reportability by managers, individual contributors and leaders within each country.
