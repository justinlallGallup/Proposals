Walton Family Foundation Opportunity Study · Year Two

Where opportunity takes root, and how it grows.

In Year One, Gallup and the Walton Family Foundation gave the country its first probability-based measure of how Americans define, access, and experience opportunity, and found that personal agency, the sense that you can shape your own path, is the strongest predictor of whether people are thriving. Year Two fields the study a second time in the same season, repeating the anchor questions to produce the first read on how opportunity is changing, and going deeper on the community conditions that decide whether people can act on it.

What Year One established

Belief in opportunity is widely shared. The conditions to act on it are not.

Year One set out to measure something the country talks about constantly but had never tracked: what opportunity means to Americans, who has access to it, and what lets it grow. Fielded among nearly 7,000 adults on the probability-based Gallup Panel, the study measured opportunity at both the individual and community levels, and gave all three of the Foundation's program areas a shared narrative rather than findings that belonged to any one team.

Its signature finding was agency. Two-thirds of Americans believe they can shape their own path, even when things are difficult, and those who feel that way are far more likely to be thriving and optimistic about the future. But agency is not evenly held. It falls sharply with income and age. Adults in the lowest-income households are far less likely than top earners to feel in control of their direction, and younger adults feel it least of all.

The more consequential finding was what agency depends on. Belief alone does not create opportunity. Whether people can act on it turns on the conditions around them, good jobs, affordable housing, quality schools, and strong community ties. Year One mapped those conditions. Year Two is where the Foundation can watch them move, and go deeper on the ones that matter most.

The Year Two approach

A second year turns a landmark into a record

A single year is a portrait. It can say where opportunity stands, but not whether it is improving, for whom, or where the Foundation's work is making a difference. Those questions can only be answered over time, and they begin with a second measurement taken the same way. How Opportunity Takes Root launched as the Foundation began a new five-year strategic plan; an annual study, fielded in the same season each year, gives that plan a public barometer.

Year Two is also the moment to go deeper for less. The instrument is designed, the panel is in place, and the media relationships are established, so the second year can add depth and sustain visibility while holding to the Year One investment. Studies that shape the national conversation are rarely one-time releases. They become the measure others cite, and opportunity in America is positioned to become that.

Reporting and communications

One flagship report, a full year of presence

Opportunity moves slowly, and a single annual report matches its pace, telling the whole story in one place with the depth a run of shorter releases cannot reach. The harder task is what comes after: turning one dataset into a sustained presence in the national conversation on opportunity, so the Foundation owns that conversation across the year rather than for a single news cycle.

The report itself carries the national picture and the first year-over-year movement, reads across income, age, and place, and pairs the data with the personal stories that make it land. A companion article on Gallup's news service takes the findings to policymakers, journalists, and the public the day it launches. But how people find and rely on information has changed. Fewer begin at a homepage or a front page; more arrive through a feed, a creator they follow, or an audio show playing in the background. A plan that reaches them in a second year has to work across all of those, not only the wire and the inbox.

So every activation is chosen to do one of two jobs, extend media reach or deepen narrative penetration, and the mix now runs from earned media and owned articles to produced video and social content, a Gallup podcast feature, and a joint creator-partnership effort with the Foundation. The content that travels furthest does more than report a number; it puts a face, a voice, or a human perspective on a finding and gives people a way to see their own role in the story, which is exactly the participation-and-voice thread the study surfaces. Sequenced across the year, each moment is timed so the story re-enters the conversation with a fresh angle rather than repeating itself, keeping the Foundation present in the weeks between the set-piece moments and not only at launch.

Several of these moments also create natural openings for grantee and community voices, if the Foundation chooses to use them, built into the sequence rather than bolted on. The launch event, the spokesperson, a mid-year convening, and a Gallup podcast feature are all designed to put a Foundation or grantee voice next to the data at the moment it is drawing attention. Why Gallup

A probability-based panel and a national platform, repeated year after year

The credibility of this work rests on how the data is gathered and where it is released. The Gallup Panel is the largest probability-based panel in the United States, recruited by random digit dialing and address-based sampling rather than opt-in, which is what lets the findings represent the country rather than the people who happened to respond. When the Foundation publishes, the work goes out through Gallup's news service, a Google News registered destination that national media cite regularly. Year One's findings were picked up across national and regional media. Fielded on the same schedule, the study becomes the record of how opportunity in America changes over the life of the Foundation's strategic plan.
