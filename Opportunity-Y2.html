Walton Family Foundation × Gallup · Opportunity Study, Year Two

Year One laid the groundwork. Year Two is a choice about the story it tells.

In Year One, Gallup and the Walton Family Foundation gave the country a national read on how Americans define, access, and experience opportunity. Year Two is a chance to build on it, and there are two strong ways forward. One sets out to shape the national conversation and establish Walton as a defining public voice on opportunity. The other concentrates the research around Walton's programs and the needs of its teams, partners, and grantees.

Common ground

What both options carry forward

Whichever option Year Two takes, it builds on the same ground: the research, the infrastructure, and the lessons of Year One, and Walton's commitment to expanding opportunity for people and places across the country.

Both options keep studying opportunity and the forces around it, community, education, work, and the conditions that let people thrive. Both produce credible, empirically grounded research on the Gallup Panel. And both preserve the most valuable threads of continuity from Year One, so the second year deepens the work rather than restarting it.

Option One · Public narrative
Shaping the National Narrative on Opportunity
Strategic ambition

Make Walton the organization America associates with opportunity: a recognizable, credible, recurring study that people come to expect, and the anchor of a national conversation about what opportunity means, who has access to it, and what shapes it. The model is the World Happiness Report, which took a concept GDP could never capture and turned it into a fixture of global policy and media. This is a narrative-building effort first. It is meant to show the country what Walton stands for, not to set program strategy.

The model, proven by Gallup

This is not a theoretical ambition. Gallup has repeatedly taken a concept once considered too abstract to measure and turned it into a recognized benchmark that shapes how leaders and the public talk about it. Gallup is also the only private-sector organization the United Nations relies on to measure several Sustainable Development Goal indicators, from financial inclusion to food insecurity.

World Happiness Report

Created with the United Nations and powered by Gallup World Poll data across 140+ countries, it gave the world a single measure of wellbeing where GDP fell short. Released each year on the UN's International Day of Happiness, its data now underpins measures in the OECD's Better Life Index and serves as a national benchmark for countries like the UAE. It remains among Gallup's most-cited research.

Gallup Well-Being Index

For a decade from 2008, Gallup measured American wellbeing every day, more than 2.5 million interviews in all, and published annual rankings of all 50 states and hundreds of communities. It helped make wellbeing a household concept and a metric that leaders and employers now track.

Employee Engagement

Gallup popularized employee engagement and built the Q12, the validated measure behind it, turning an abstract idea into something leaders everywhere now manage. Its annual State of the Global Workplace report tracks engagement across more than 140 countries and sets the terms for how organizations talk about their people.

Primary audience

National and regional media, policymakers and civic leaders, researchers and thought leaders, philanthropic peers, community leaders, and the broader public. This option is built to be heard well beyond Walton's own networks.

Definition of success

A recognizable public narrative that gives Walton a distinctive, enduring place in the national conversation on opportunity. Success looks like credible coverage and narrative uptake, growing recognition of Walton's read on opportunity, external leaders drawing on it, and real anticipation of each release. It is explicitly not a tool for program strategy, and it is not designed to serve each program team, who already have deeper research for their own agendas. One guardrail matters: the study should still surface the issues Walton works on, so it never reads as out of touch, but it is measured by narrative reach, not by a guaranteed number of stories.

How Option One works

The work begins not with a survey, but with defining the construct itself. Everything after that is designed around the public story it is meant to create.

1
Define the construct

Desk research, sessions with outside experts, and a close read of Year One settle what opportunity means and how to measure it, before a single survey question is written.

2
Set the North Star

One clear, defensible signature measure, asked the same way every year. The World Happiness Report model: a single, understandable number, not a complicated index.

3
Make it comprehensive

Build the full measurement around that North Star so it captures Opportunity in the round: its core dimensions, the outcomes people pursue, and how both differ across people and places.

4
Lead the conversation

A flagship annual report and a year of amplification make Walton the name the public and the media attach to Opportunity.

Relationship to Year One

Year One produced a strong, credible read on how Americans experience Opportunity and the conditions that shape it. Option One builds on that work and sharpens the aim: one defined measure of Opportunity, and a fuller account of what it means to people in different places. Reaching that bar means examining some forces beyond Walton's current funding areas, and that breadth is a strength rather than a stretch. It positions Walton as a credible voice on Opportunity wherever it takes root, lets the Foundation point to partners working in adjacent fields, and never requires Walton to claim ownership of every issue the research touches.

Long-term potential

The opportunity counterpart to the World Happiness Report: a recognizable annual study others cite, anticipated each year, that makes Walton synonymous with credible insight into opportunity and, over time, helps set the terms of the national conversation.

Option Two · Program-aligned
Program-Aligned Opportunity Research
Strategic ambition

Continue and strengthen the Year One model by putting Walton's program priorities and information needs at the center of the research. This option produces a more targeted body of data for Walton's program teams, its grantees and partners, and the field leaders working in its priority areas. It is designed first for program relevance and practical use, rather than for broad public positioning.

Primary audience

Walton leadership and program teams, grantees, program partners, practitioners and field stakeholders, and the specialized audiences tied to Walton's funding areas.

Definition of success

Useful, credible, program-aligned data that helps Walton and its partners understand the experiences, attitudes, needs, and conditions related to Walton's existing work. Success shows up as relevance to program teams, usefulness to grantees and partners, application in program communications and field engagement, strong data on priority issues, and the ability to deepen and contextualize work already underway, with continuity and improvement over Year One. Media coverage may still happen, but it is not the primary design objective or the main measure of success.

Research and design process

A more straightforward extension of Year One. It begins with structured input from program teams, identifies the questions and topics most relevant to current priorities, preserves the Year One measures worth tracking for trend analysis, refines or retires the topics that did not earn their place, and builds the instrument around program needs. Findings are then organized for the specific stakeholder groups and fields that will use them. Unlike the narrative option, it does not require an extended phase to define a universal public measure of opportunity, so it can move more directly into prioritization, survey design, data collection, and reporting.

Relationship to Year One

This is the direct continuation of Year One. Opportunity stays the organizing theme, and Year One's measures carry forward for trend analysis, but the research concentrates on the topics most relevant to Walton's portfolio. The final product is still more focused than Year One; its coherence comes from alignment with Walton's work rather than from a single public-facing construct.

Long-term potential

A durable, program-centered research series that becomes a reliable instrument for Walton and its partners: deepening year over year, tracking how attitudes and conditions move across Walton's priority areas, and strengthening the evidence base that program teams and grantees rely on.
