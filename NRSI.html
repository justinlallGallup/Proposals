The NobleReach Service Index

An annual Gallup measure of how Americans understand public service, how highly they regard it, and who feels called to it, across sectors and at every stage of a career.

This proposal describes a partnership between NobleReach Foundation and Gallup to create the index: a national measure built on the Gallup Panel, released publicly each year, and designed to become the reference point for how the country talks about public service. It sets out what the index would accomplish, how it comes together, how it would be developed and launched, options for scope, a timeline to a summer 2027 release, and examples of how Gallup has built and launched measures like it.

What the index will accomplish

Americans are regularly asked what they think of government. Far less is known about how they think of public service itself: what it means to them, how highly they regard it, and whether they see themselves in it. Organizations working in this space have a handful of data points, but none that settle these questions.

The NobleReach Service Index would give the country that measure. Built on the Gallup Panel, released publicly each year, and asked the same way each time, it is designed to become the official statistic on how Americans view and pursue public service, and a recurring moment for NobleReach to bring government, industry, and academia together around new evidence.

1

A national baseline

A 2027 reading of how Americans understand and value public service, and how many feel a professional drive to serve, in any sector.

2

An official statistic

An annual figure on a fixed release schedule that media, researchers, and policymakers come to expect, cite, and track.

3

A clearer picture of who serves

How early-career adults and current and former government workers compare with the public, including the dual citizens who carry a mission across sectors.

4

A platform for NobleReach

New evidence each year to anchor NobleReach’s convenings, inform its programs, and give its partners a common reference point.

What existing research covers, and what it leaves open

What existing research covers

  • Views of the federal workforce and confidence in government institutions
  • Support for national service programs and service years
  • How Americans rate leaders across sectors, including the U.S. News Best Leaders research NobleReach supports
  • Civic participation and volunteering, including the Kettering Foundation and Gallup Democracy for All Project
  • How Americans view military service and veterans, including Gallup’s work with With Honor

What no current measure provides

  • A recurring national reading of how Americans understand and value public service as a profession, in any sector
  • The prevalence of the drive to serve, and what shapes it
  • A consistent way to describe and compare the people who act on that drive

Gallup’s review to date finds adjacent data but no measure that gets at these questions directly. Confirming where the gaps are is the first task of the work.

Developing the index

The specifics are set with NobleReach in the first weeks of the work. Below are the sample design, the process Gallup follows, and the questions on the table.

Who will be surveyed

General population

5,000 to 10,000 U.S. adults

A probability-based sample from the Gallup Panel, weighted to be representative of the U.S. adult population.

Early-career adults

500 to 1,000 additional

Adults ages 18 to 34, the years when many people choose a career path and a sector.

Current and former government workers

500 to 1,000 additional

Adults who work in federal, state, or local government today or have in the past, including those who have moved to another sector: the dual citizens at the center of NobleReach’s work.

About the Gallup Panel. A probability-based panel of approximately 100,000 U.S. adults recruited through address-based sampling and random-digit dialing. Members cannot opt in. Fielding runs four to six weeks. Added samples are drawn using Panel member profiles and screening, and Gallup confirms the feasibility of each target during design. Final sample sizes are set once the research questions are settled; the options in the scope builder bracket the likely range.

The process, from landscape review to official statisticSix steps

What the index measures is not decided in advance. It is the product of a process Gallup has used to build national measures for decades, from its trends on American life dating to the 1930s to the indices in the Gallup World Poll and partner measures such as the Democracy for All Project. Each step is done with NobleReach.

1

Start with what is known

Gallup researchers review the published literature and Gallup’s own data on civic participation, veterans and leadership, Gen Z aspirations, and the Washington region, and map what each covers. The result is a recommendation to NobleReach: what existing data can already tell you, which questions nothing answers, and where new measurement would add the most.

OutputLandscape review and gap recommendation
2

Define the constructs together

Working sessions with NobleReach settle what the index is measuring. Public service as NobleReach defines it is professional, paid work at any level of government, with open questions about government-funded nonprofits and companies working directly with government on national challenges. Rather than fix those boundaries in advance, the research measures each form of work separately and lets Americans’ responses show where they place the line.

OutputAgreed definitions and research questions
3

Develop and test the items

Gallup drafts the questionnaire and NobleReach reviews it at whatever depth it prefers, from high-level direction to line-by-line edits. Items are written to measure the values, motivations, and experiences beneath partisan views of government, so that results do not collapse onto party lines. Cognitive interviews confirm that respondents read each item as intended before anything is fielded.

OutputTested survey instrument
4

Field on the Gallup Panel

A web survey of Gallup Panel members over four to six weeks, followed by quality review and weighting so results represent the U.S. adult population. Added samples allow early-career adults and current and former government workers to be reported on their own.

OutputWeighted dataset
5

Analyze, write, and release

Gallup scores the index, identifies what drives it, and profiles the groups that matter most. Gallup drafts the report and articles; NobleReach reviews before release. The findings can be released at once around a launch moment, or in sequence over several months, a decision made at the start of the work.

OutputFlagship report, articles, public release
6

Establish the official statistic

Each annual wave asks the core questions the same way, with a fresh cross-section of the Panel, and adds new ones as public questions change. From the second wave, the index shows change over time rather than a single snapshot. Released on a fixed schedule, it becomes the official statistic on how Americans view and pursue public service: the figure media, researchers, and policymakers look for each year.

OutputAn annual official statistic on public service
Questions the research could take upFive areas

These are the questions raised in our conversation. Steps 1 and 2 determine which of them existing data already answers, which the index should carry as core questions each year, and which belong in a rotating module.

Perceptions
What do Americans picture when they hear “public service,” and how highly do they regard it?
Whether military service and community volunteering come to mind before careers in government; which forms of work people count; the values they associate with service; and where their knowledge of public service careers is thin.
Sentiment
How widespread is the drive to serve, and where is it concentrated?
The share of Americans who feel a professional drive toward mission-driven work, whether or not they work in government today; how that varies by age and career stage; and whether there are geographic concentrations, as Gallup’s research in the Washington region suggests there may be.
Drivers
What shapes that drive, and how early does it start?
Family exposure to government work, a specific cause, a sense of duty, education and early experiences; what role mission plays in career decisions alongside pay and stability; and what keeps interested people from acting.
The people who serve
What distinguishes dual citizens from everyone else?
Whether people who serve or have served, wherever they now work, differ in wellbeing, sense of mission, and what they value beyond income; how their careers move between sectors; and what the rest of the public can learn from them.
Definition
Where does the public draw the boundary of service?
Whether Americans count government-funded nonprofits and mission-driven companies working directly with government as public service, measured for each form separately.

Launch and amplification

A measure earns its standing in public. Gallup pairs the research with an in-house communications team and a release plan built around a launch moment and a sustained calendar. The scope builder below turns these into selectable options.

From Gallup’s headquarters to the front pages

Research built at Gallup, convened at Gallup events and global convenings, carried on Gallup’s own channels, and amplified by the press.

How Gallup Strategic Communications turns a release into a national conversationEight channels

Gallup’s own channels

Articles on news.gallup.com and posts on Gallup’s social channels, where the public already reads survey findings, each carrying NobleReach’s name.

Earned media

Embargoed briefings and pitching to national and policy press, with interview preparation for NobleReach spokespeople and the option of a satellite media tour on release day.

Launch moments

A launch at Gallup World Headquarters in Washington, D.C., where Gallup convenes policymakers, agency leaders, university partners, and media around new research, as it did for the Democracy for All Project.

National stages

Gallup pursues placement for partner research at the national convenings where it already presents, including the Aspen Ideas Festival, and can host a gathering alongside a convening. Programming decisions rest with the organizers.

A sustained calendar

Follow-on articles and thematic reports released over the months after launch, so the index stays in circulation between annual waves.

NobleReach’s network

Roundtables, private briefings, and webinars that put the data in front of the government, industry, and academic partners NobleReach convenes, with Gallup researchers presenting.

Content made to travel

Static and short video pieces for Gallup and NobleReach channels, designed for social platforms and built from the findings.

Data people can explore

An optional index explorer lets journalists, researchers, and partners look at scores by group, extending the life of each wave.
