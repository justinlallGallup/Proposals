In brief

What Gallup proposes

Gallup proposes a partnership with Mozilla Foundation to create the Global Imagination Index: a credible, trusted, and globally comparable indicator of the state of imagination around the world.

Today there is no shared way to measure imagination across countries and populations. That leaves a data gap at the center of one of the defining conversations of the AI era, and no common evidence base for the governments, funders, and companies that want to invest in it.

The Index would be developed together, and built in sequence. Gallup and Mozilla Foundation would first define the underlying construct through desk research and a convening of leading experts, then design and validate a survey instrument, then measure at global scale on the Gallup World Poll, and finally construct the Index and bring it into the world. What the Index should ultimately capture is not presumed here; defining it well is the first task of the work.

This is work Gallup has done before. The life-evaluation data behind the World Happiness Report and the World Bank's Global Findex are both collected on the Gallup World Poll. Gallup proposes to bring that same rigor, reach, and credibility to imagination.

The opportunity

Fill the data gap. Inform the global conversation.

As AI makes many forms of production cheaper and more accessible, the decisive question is shifting: not only what people can make, but who gets to imagine, exercise agency, and shape what comes next.

Imagination is a human and societal capacity: the ability to envisage alternatives, and to turn them into reality. It runs far wider than the creative industries, through technology, education, public institutions, economic participation, and the ways societies decide their own futures. Technology can widen those possibilities. Through unequal access, concentrated ownership, and dependence on closed platforms, it can also narrow them.

Yet there is no accepted way to see any of this clearly across the world. Imagination and creativity are counted mostly by outputs: patents, exports, box-office receipts. None of these capture whether a person feels able to imagine and build, or whether the conditions around them make it possible. That absence is the opportunity.

This is Mozilla Foundation's own terrain. In its framing, imagination is a human and societal capacity to envisage alternatives, and the imagination economy is the set of conditions that lets people turn those possibilities into reality. It is a thesis Mozilla Foundation carries through its work to imagine, co-create, and mobilize human-centered technology, and through its conviction that imagination deserves to be treated as core infrastructure, not a byproduct. Gallup's role is to make that thesis measurable.

The ambition is to make imagination legible: something a government, a funder, or a company can see, compare, and choose to invest in.

The shared premise behind this partnership

A credible, trusted indicator would do for imagination what trusted measures have done for wellbeing and financial inclusion: anchor the global conversation, reveal where capacity and opportunity differ across populations and geographies, and show decision-makers where investment is most needed. Exactly what the Index should measure, and how far it draws on people's own experience versus the conditions around them, is not yet settled. Resolving that, together, is the first and most important part of the work.

How we would accomplish it

Build the construct together, then measure it at scale

A credible index is earned in sequence. The partnership moves from definition, to a tested instrument, to global measurement, to a public measure others can use. Each phase has a clear decision point, so Mozilla Foundation can commit to scale only once the foundations are proven.

Instrument design and validation

With the framework agreed, Gallup turns it into a working instrument: a concise set of survey items that measure the construct faithfully and travel across languages and cultures. Items are cognitively tested in several countries, checked for measurement invariance, and piloted, then finalized as a concise module ready for the World Poll. The result is an instrument that means the same thing in every place it is asked.

Global deployment and flagship launch

In the first quarter of 2028, the module extends to the full annual Gallup World Poll across more than 140 countries, representing over 98% of the world's adult population. Gallup builds the composite Index at global scale, analyzes how capacity and opportunity differ across populations and geographies, and anchors the major launches. The 20-country quarterly read can continue alongside it, giving Mozilla Foundation a frequent pulse between annual global waves.

Phase 1 · The foundation

Framework and indicator development

This is where the Index is won or lost, and where the construct is actually built. Phase 1 begins with a deliberate period of desk research, then brings external and internal experts together to shape the measure. Its output is a defensible framework the field can stand behind, before any measurement commits resources to it.

  • 1

    Desk research: a landscape review of what already exists

    Gallup begins with a structured review of measures adjacent to imagination, from innovation and creativity indices to human-capability and cultural-participation frameworks, alongside the academic literature on imagination, creative self-efficacy, and agency. The aim is to learn from what is already measured, map the conditions that could feed the Index, and find the space only a population measure can fill. This directly answers Mozilla Foundation's interest in understanding existing measures and where survey data adds something new.

  • 2

    A working definition of the construct

    From that review, Gallup drafts a first definition of imagination as this Index might measure it, and a set of candidate dimensions. This gives experts something concrete to react to rather than an abstract conversation, and it is explicitly a starting point for debate, not a conclusion.

  • 3

    Expert input, external and internal

    Gallup then convenes the people who should shape the measure: external scholars and practitioners drawn from Mozilla Foundation's network and the wider field, together with Gallup's own methodologists, behavioral scientists, and World Poll regional directors. Their task is to challenge the definition, confirm what matters across cultures, and decide what the Index should and should not try to capture.

  • 4

    A validated framework and measurement blueprint

    Phase 1 closes with an agreed conceptual framework, a prioritized indicator architecture, and a blueprint for how far the Index draws on survey data versus other indicators. This becomes the reference document for everything that follows, and a credible artifact Mozilla Foundation can share to signal leadership on the idea.

Why Gallup

Gallup has built the measures the world trusts

Establishing a new global indicator is difficult and rare. Gallup has done it repeatedly, supplying the comparable, individual-level data beneath some of the world's most-cited measures, and bringing the rigor and convening authority to make a new one credible from the start.

World Happiness Report

Turning wellbeing into a number the world watches

The report's central life-evaluation ranking is built on the Gallup World Poll. The report itself is produced by the Wellbeing Research Centre at the University of Oxford with the UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network; Gallup provides the comparable global data beneath it.

A subjective human measure, made globally comparable
World Bank · Global Findex

The reference measure of financial inclusion

The World Bank's Global Findex, the standard source on who can access and use financial services, is collected through the Gallup World Poll. It shows how a composite global measure built on Gallup data becomes essential infrastructure for policy and investment.

A composite index built on World Poll collection
Gallup Global Emotions

Measuring what statistics usually miss

Gallup's annual reading of positive and negative experience shows that human states most assume are unmeasurable can in fact be tracked rigorously, year over year, across the whole world. Imagination belongs in that company.

Intangible human experience, tracked at global scale

Enabling conditions can be assembled from existing data. The lived experience of imagination and agency cannot: it has to be asked, of real people, comparably, everywhere. Since 2005 the Gallup World Poll has surveyed individuals in more than 140 countries each year, representing over 98% of the world's adult population, using probability-based methods that make results comparable across borders. It is the only platform that can measure the human side of imagination globally, the same way in every country.

The invitation

Make imagination legible for the whole world.

Mozilla Foundation has named the idea and built the movement around it. Gallup can give it a measure: rigorous, global, and human. The first step is small, contained, and valuable in itself: develop the framework, with the field, and establish the construct that everything else depends on.
