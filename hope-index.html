Establishing the Global HOPE Index

The world's official measure of financial dignity

For more than three decades, Operation HOPE has made financial dignity its goal: the confidence, control and self-determination that turn check-cashing customers into bank customers, renters into homeowners, and small business dreamers into small business owners. The next step is to make financial dignity a measured, comparable fact about the whole world. Gallup proposes the Global HOPE Index: a Financial Dignity construct carried onto the Gallup World Poll, the platform that already produces the world's official statistics on hunger, financial inclusion and modern slavery. Together they position Operation HOPE and John Hope Bryant as the definitive global voice on financial dignity, and carry the silver rights movement onto the world stage.

The opportunityFrom a movement to a global statistic

The world measures money everywhere and dignity almost nowhere. There is no comparable, continuous read on whether people feel in command of their financial lives, whether they believe free enterprise works for them and not just the fortunate few, whether they can imagine building something and passing it on. Operation HOPE can create that measure and own it.

Gallup and Operation HOPE have built a measure of financial dignity together before, so the partnership starts with a track record. The Global HOPE Index is a new and independent measure, built for the whole world from the start. Financial dignity is a measurable human condition. It describes whether a person feels in command of their financial life, believes the system gives them a fair chance to get ahead, and can imagine building something worth passing on to the next generation. It marks the difference between surviving and thriving, and it follows a human pattern rather than an American one.

Human patterns are exactly what the Gallup World Poll was built to measure. For twenty years the World Poll has asked the same carefully translated questions of people in 140 countries, producing the official global statistics that governments and international institutions rely on. Placing a Financial Dignity construct on that platform turns a decades-old idea into the reference point for its field, cited by the World Bank, the United Nations, central banks, foundations and the global press.

The timing is deliberate. Operation HOPE announces the program from the stage of the Hope Global Forum in Atlanta this December. From there, Gallup delivers a quarterly global read across 20 markets through 2027 and the full Global HOPE Index worldwide in 2028. This gives the movement something no campaign can generate on its own: a rigorous, comparable read on financial dignity, delivered on a calendar the world already watches.

Exactly what the Index measures, and how far it draws on people's own experience, is defined together at the start of the work. Building that construct, with Operation HOPE and the field, is the first phase of the partnership, not a conclusion presumed here.

How the data is collected

Samples are drawn from the latest census or national statistics office in each country. Depending on infrastructure, Gallup fields face to face, by telephone or online, applying more than 90 years of experience across questionnaire design, sampling, data collection, quality assurance, and weighting. Because the same instrument is fielded the same way each year, results are comparable across countries and over time. That comparability is the asset a global measure of financial dignity needs and cannot manufacture on its own.

Reaching the people finance forgets

Face to face interviewers reach the unbanked, the rural and the informally employed, the very populations most digital financial surveys miss and the ones Operation HOPE exists to serve. A single study runs across three collection modes, matched to what produces the most representative sample in each market.

  • Face to face (CAPI)Emerging and unbanked markets
  • Telephone (CATI)Developed and connected markets
  • WebHigh-penetration digital markets
Official statisticsThe platform that writes the world's official numbers

Around the world, the job of collecting official statistics falls to national statistical offices, and many are under mounting pressure as demand for high-quality data grows. Gallup helps bridge that gap. The World Poll already carries three United Nations Sustainable Development Goal indicators. Financial dignity can become the next global statistic measured to the same standard, sitting alongside the financial-inclusion data the World Bank already sources from the same platform.

How Gallup bridges the data gap

  • 2

How we would accomplish it

We build the measure, then take it to the world

A credible global index is earned in stages. First, Gallup and Operation HOPE define the construct together. Next, Gallup turns it into a validated instrument. Then Gallup fields it quarterly across 20 markets in 2027, and finally across the whole world in 2028. Each phase produces something of value on its own, so Operation HOPE builds toward the full measure with proof at every step.

The program at a glance
Phase 1

Define the construct

Gallup and Operation HOPE define the Financial Dignity construct together, using desk research and expert input to agree exactly what the Index measures.

Phase 2

Instrument and validation

Gallup turns the agreed construct into survey items, translates them, and cognitively tests them across cultures so the module reads the same way everywhere.

Phase 3 · 2027

Quarterly global read

Gallup fields the module across 20 strategic markets every quarter through 2027, which produces the first comparable read of financial dignity worldwide.

Phase 4 · 2028

Full wave and launch

Gallup carries the construct onto the full 140-country World Poll wave in 2028, builds the Global HOPE Index, and launches it to the world.
