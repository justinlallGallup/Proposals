Establishing the world's official statistics on kindness

In 2026, the Values in Action and Gallup partnership put a number on kindness in America. The next step is to make kindness a measured, comparable fact about the whole world. Gallup proposes to carry the kindness module onto the Gallup World Poll, the platform that already produces the world's official statistics on hunger, financial inclusion and modern slavery, and to make Values in Action the definitive global source on how kindness is lived across every region of the planet.

The opportunity

From a national conversation to a global statistic

The 2026 State of Kindness in America study did something rare: it turned a value everyone talks about into evidence leaders can act on. The next move is to ask the world the same questions, and to keep asking them, year after year.

The findings from the first year were clear and quietly powerful. A majority of Americans see kindness around them and have felt it themselves in the past week. Most believe their fellow citizens are kind. And the study surfaced a mechanism worth understanding at scale: the more people experience and witness kindness, the more comfortable they are extending it to strangers, and the more they believe their country is kind. Measured this way, kindness behaves less like a mood and more like a current that spreads.

That mechanism is not uniquely American. It is a human pattern, and human patterns are exactly what the Gallup World Poll was built to measure. For twenty years the World Poll has asked the same carefully translated questions of people in more than 140 countries, producing the official global statistics that governments and international institutions rely on. No comparable, continuous measure of kindness exists anywhere in that record. Values in Action is positioned to create it, and to own it.

The timing is deliberate. The United States of Kindness framed 2026 around America's 250th anniversary and a call for 250 acts of kindness. Extending the measure worldwide in 2027 turns a national anniversary into a global story, and gives the movement something no campaign can generate on its own: a rigorous, comparable read on kindness in the places where a genuinely global conversation happens.

Global surveys should have been instituted many years ago by the World Bank or the United Nations, but it took a private sector organization to succeed where the international organizations had failed so badly.

The finding worth taking global

Experience kindness

Receiving and witnessing kind acts in everyday life.

Extend it further

Comfort initiating kindness toward strangers rises sharply. Among Americans who felt kindness many times in the past week, roughly eight in ten are very comfortable being kind to strangers.

Believe in a kinder country

People who see kindness are far more likely to describe their nation as kind. The perception of kindness tracks the experience of it.

35% · 64%
A generational gap worth watching worldwide. Just 35% of Americans aged 18 to 29 are very comfortable being kind to strangers, against 64% of those 65 and older. A global measure would show whether that gap is an American story or a generational one shared across cultures.
The kindness module

Four questions, asked the same way everywhere

The U.S. module runs to roughly two minutes of interview time. Gallup's questionnaire scientists adapt each item for cross-cultural comparability, translating and cognitively testing wording so that a respondent in Jakarta, Lagos or Warsaw is answering the same underlying question as one in Ohio. The items below carry the U.S. study directly into a global frame.

How the data is collected

Nationally representative samples are drawn from the latest census or national statistics office in each country. Depending on infrastructure, Gallup fields face to face, by telephone or online, applying more than 90 years of experience across questionnaire design, sampling, data collection, quality assurance, and weighting. Because the same instrument is fielded the same way each year, results are comparable across countries and over time. That comparability is the asset a global kindness measure needs and cannot manufacture on its own.

Official statistics

The platform that writes the world's official numbers

Around the world, the job of collecting official statistics falls to national statistical offices, and many are under mounting pressure as demand for high-quality data grows. Gallup helps bridge that gap. The World Poll already carries three United Nations Sustainable Development Goal indicators. Kindness can become the next global statistic measured to the same standard.

How Gallup bridges the data gap

  • 1
    Developing and mainstreaming new indicators for issues the world has never measured consistently. Kindness is exactly this kind of new indicator.
  • 2
    Collecting comparable data in the many countries and years where national statistics are not otherwise available.
  • 3
    Validating and strengthening measurement frameworks, so a new measure holds up to scrutiny from researchers and institutions.
  • 4
    Reaching populations traditional research misses, through a network of local research partners in every region.
  • 5
    Running the field experiments and quality controls that keep a global measure accurate year after year.
