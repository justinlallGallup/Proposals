Executive summary — prepared for The Rockefeller Foundation

The AI Worker Pulse

An annual, 50-state study of how AI is changing work for American workers — reported state by state, and repeated every year.

Why it exists

The worker experience of AI, measured the same way every year

Most polling on AI measures adoption, or captures national sentiment once and moves on. What no one tracks consistently is how workers themselves are faring — their security, their agency, and their capacity to make the transition — reported the same way and repeated year over year. The AI Worker Pulse is that standing measure: a national, probability-based read on the Gallup Panel that carries the public headline, with the option to extend it to every state for leaders who need the picture where policy is actually made.

What exists
Adoption & macro data

Robust tracking of who uses AI, how often, and in which tools — plus labor-market aggregates.

What's missing
The worker-side signal

Security, agency, and — above all — whether workers can actually make the transition. Measured consistently over time.

What's absent entirely
A geographic lens

Where disruption lands, and how it differs by region and industry — the cut no national poll provides.

What it measures

Illustrative focus areas

The instrument is built with the Foundation, but the areas below are where a worker-centric study adds the most for policymakers — starting with transition capacity, the question almost no one is measuring.

Transition capacity

The real ability to retrain, redeploy, and move across sectors. Whether a worker knows what skills a transition would require, can actually access retraining, what stands in the way — structural barriers and personal ones — and whether a realistic path exists across roles and sectors. Tracked over time, and cut by region and industry, so the country can see who is able to move, not just who is exposed.

Worker dignity & agency

Whether AI is done with workers or to them: voice in how it is introduced, autonomy, trust in the systems, and exposure to monitoring — and whether that experience holds for all workers, not just higher-paid knowledge workers.

Economic security

Job-loss concern, wage pressure, and the financial resilience to absorb a disruption — the exposure workers feel and the cushion they actually have.

The recommended design

35,000 interviews a year, every state

A national Gallup Panel study at n=10,000 — probability-based — plus state-level collection on consumer panels at n=500 in each of the 50 states (25,000). A 15-minute annual survey carries the flagship study; a 3-minute quarterly module keeps the signal current between annual waves.

On method: the national headline comes from the probability-based Gallup Panel, the largest of its kind in the United States. State-level estimates come from consumer panels weighted to Census benchmarks, with response quality audited and urban/rural balance stipulated. The quarterly module is fielded on the Gallup Panel, so change can be tracked over time alongside the national read.
