The Rockefeller-Gallup AI Initiative

Illustrative name — final branding to be developed

Designed to be the primary source on how AI is reshaping work for American workers — the read the country's leaders rely on to act. Nationally representative, reported for every kind of worker and every state, and repeated year after year.

Why it exists

The worker experience of AI, measured the same way every year

Most AI-and-work coverage lives in two places: national aggregates, and a narrow slice of — often knowledge — workers. What's missing is nuance: which workers, where, and how they actually feel. Because the national sample is large enough to report on each kind of worker — full-time, self-employed, gig, small-employer, large-employer, and the non-employed — the study can show how AI is reshaping work for all workers, not just the ones the story usually features: where it is landing, how workers feel about it, and the voice of every kind of worker. That is its unique contribution to public understanding — a nationally representative, multi-year benchmark on the Gallup Panel, with the option to extend it to every state.

What it measures

Illustrative focus areas

The instrument is built with the Foundation, but the areas below are where a worker-centric study adds the most for policymakers — starting with transition capacity, the question almost no one is measuring.

Transition capacity

The real ability to retrain, redeploy, and move across sectors. Whether a worker knows what skills a transition would require, can actually access retraining, what stands in the way — structural barriers and personal ones — and whether a realistic path exists across roles and sectors. Tracked over time, and cut by region and industry, so the country can see who is able to move, not just who is exposed.

Worker dignity & agency

Whether AI is done with workers or to them: voice in how it is introduced, autonomy, trust in the systems, and exposure to monitoring — and whether that experience holds for all workers, not just higher-paid knowledge workers.

Economic security

Job-loss concern, wage pressure, and the financial resilience to absorb a disruption — the exposure workers feel and the cushion they actually have.

The recommended design

35,000 interviews a year, every state

A national Gallup Panel study at n=10,000 — nationally representative, probability-based — plus state-level collection on consumer panels at n=500 in each of the 50 states (25,000). The 10,000 is large enough to report on each kind of worker with confidence — full-time employed, self-employed, gig, and small- vs. large-employer workers — and by industry, not just workers in the aggregate. It covers both employed and non-employed adults (a prime-age 25–64 focus, with early-career adults 20–24 included), capturing the AI-displaced and discouraged workers the headline unemployment rate misses. A 15-minute annual flagship instrument, offered in English and Spanish, carries the study, with a 3-minute quarterly tracking module that captures the measures moving fast between waves.

On method: the national headline comes from the nationally representative, probability-based Gallup Panel, the largest of its kind in the United States. State-level estimates come from consumer panels weighted to Census benchmarks, with response quality audited. The 10,000-person national sample is sized so results report with confidence by worker type — gig, self-employed, and small- vs. large-employer workers — and by industry.

About Gallup

The standing to make this the number of record

Gallup has reported the will of the people for more than 90 years and is the world's leading authority on the workplace. When newsrooms, boardrooms, and statehouses need the truth about work, Gallup is where they look first — a standing this study inherits on day one. Behind it sits a deep bench on the economics of work and AI.
