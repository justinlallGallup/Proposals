A Four-Year Continuation Proposal · Gallup and PetSmart Charities

The next four years of the State of Pet Care

The PetSmart Charities–Gallup State of Pet Care Study began with a simple aim: to quantify, for the first time, how families and the professionals who serve them experience access to veterinary care. The pet parent baseline landed in 2025; the veterinarian perspective followed in early 2026. This proposal continues that established program, extending it across four years, adding qualitative depth, and covering both the United States and Canada. Investment is confirmed by year and broken out by component and region.

The work so far

What the research has established

Two findings from the studies completed to date, and the basis for the waves that follow.

52%
of U.S. pet parents had skipped or declined needed veterinary care in the past year, most often over cost. From the 2025 pet parent baseline, fielded on the Gallup Panel with roughly 2,500 dog and cat owners.
94%
of U.S. veterinarians say clients’ finances limit the care they can provide. From the 2026 veterinarian study, the second in the series, with more than 900 practicing vets.

The State of Pet Care Study was built to do more than describe a moment. PetSmart Charities framed it as a baseline to monitor change, part of a $100 million commitment to expand access to veterinary care for the roughly 50 million pets in the U.S. that go without the care they need. The first two chapters covered pet parents in 2025 and veterinarians in 2026, each released through Gallup’s news service and reported across national media. What a baseline cannot do on its own is show a direction over time. That is the purpose of the next four years.

The continuation keeps the established program running and extends it in three ways: it repeats each study so a baseline becomes a trend, it adds qualitative interviews that add context to the statistics, and it covers both the United States and Canada. The cadence alternates by design. Pet parents are surveyed in Year 1, veterinarians in Year 2, then each again in Years 3 and 4, so a release stays in market every year, each wave informs the next, and investment spreads evenly across the partnership. The workstreams are described below, followed by the roadmap and the investment, broken out by component and region.

Workstream One · Years 1 & 3

The Pet Parent Survey

A representative measure of how pet owners experience veterinary care, fielded in the U.S. and Canada and repeated in Year 3 to extend the trend. Each pet parent wave includes the qualitative interviews described in Workstream Three.

Quantitative · pet owners

Access to veterinary care

United StatesCanada

Gallup works with the PetSmart Charities team to develop the survey items, program and test the instrument, field it, weight the data to correct for nonresponse, and deliver a deidentified dataset, crosstabulations and a publicly releasable report. The design carries forward the questions established in prior waves so that results read as a continuous trend, with special attention to the groups PetSmart Charities' veterinary programs focus on.

United States

  • Fielded on the Gallup Panel, the largest probability-based panel of U.S. adults (established 2004, 100,000+ members), so results are representative of the national pet-owner population
  • Web and mail administration, so offline adults, who are disproportionately older and rural, remain in the sample for subgroup analysis
  • Minimum 2,000 pet owners per wave, sized for reporting by education, age, race and ethnicity, income and community type
  • Mail survey offered in a Spanish-language version for households that prefer it
  • Data weighted to reflect the national pet-owner population

Canada

  • Fielded via an opt-in online panel and random-digit-dial telephone, with the instrument mirrored from the U.S. wave so the two can be read alongside one another
  • Minimum 2,000 pet owners per wave, sized for national reporting and core subgroups
  • Cross-country comparisons framed with appropriate calibration, since the Canadian sample and the U.S. Gallup Panel are distinct instruments

Workstream Two · Years 2 & 4

The Veterinarian Survey

How the veterinarians who deliver care view capacity, cost and the barriers their clients face. The 2026 study reached more than 900 U.S. veterinarians; the continuation repeats it and extends it to Canada.

Quantitative · veterinarians

The veterinarian perspective

United StatesCanada

Veterinarians are surveyed by web, which Gallup finds the most effective way to reach and screen this hard-to-identify profession. Recruitment uses a hybrid model: sample lists PetSmart Charities can provide through partners and associations, supplemental sample from Gallup's panel provider to fulfill quotas, and a secure pre-registration link PetSmart Charities can share with veterinarians in its own network for Gallup to invite directly. A veterinarian honorarium is included in the fee for every respondent, whatever their recruitment source, and the questionnaire is aligned with the pet parent instrument so the two studies can be read together.

United States

  • 750 guaranteed completes per wave, sized for deeper subgroup analysis and stronger statistical power
  • Hybrid recruitment across client-provided lists, supplemental panel sample and client-sourced pre-registration
  • 10–12 minute instrument, mobile-optimized, aligned to the pet parent survey
  • Veterinarian honorarium included in the fee

Canada

  • 350 guaranteed completes per wave
  • Network-based sampling through vetted Canadian veterinary networks, alongside panel sample, to ensure coverage
  • Veterinarian honorarium included in the fee

Workstream Three · With each pet parent wave

The Qualitative Interviews

Twenty in-depth conversations with pet parents that add context to the survey findings and, with consent, make participants available to journalists at launch. Included with each pet parent wave, in Years 1 and 3.

Qualitative · pet owners

In-depth interviews with pet parents

United StatesCanada

A trained Gallup moderator conducts twenty in-person, hour-long interviews across four markets, five in each, recruited with PetSmart Charities' partners in each community. Every interview is recorded and transcribed with consent; the transcripts are analyzed thematically and woven into the public report, adding depth the survey alone cannot. Participants are asked to consent to media recontact, so that consenting pet parents can be made available to journalists covering the study at launch, providing reporters with people whose experiences match the findings.

United States 10 interviews, 2 markets

  • Two U.S. markets, five interviews in each
  • In-person facilitation in English, roughly 60 minutes per session
  • Recording, verbatim transcription and secure U.S.-compliant storage
  • Recruitment screener and coordination with local partners

Canada 10 interviews, 2 markets

  • Two Canadian markets, five interviews in each
  • In-person facilitation, roughly 60 minutes per session
  • Recording, verbatim transcription and secure Canada-compliant storage
  • Media recontact consent captured at recruitment and again at close

Why Gallup

How the research is built and released

The credibility of this program rests on how the data is gathered and where it is released. In the U.S., the Gallup Panel is the largest probability-based panel of adults in the country, not an opt-in list, which is what makes the findings representative of the population rather than of who happened to respond. When PetSmart Charities chooses to publish, the work can be released through Gallup's news service, a Google News–registered destination that reaches well over a million readers a month and is regularly cited across national outlets. The program's first two chapters were reported nationally and referenced across the veterinary profession. Repeated over four years, that record documents how access to veterinary care changes over time.
