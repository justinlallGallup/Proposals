Partnership Concept Note  ·  Gallup × Skoll Foundation

Amplifying the impact of the world's social innovators

Gallup is eager to partner with the Skoll Foundation to put credible global evidence behind the social entrepreneurs Skoll champions, strengthen the stories told at the Skoll World Forum, and give the wider social impact community a shared conversation to rally around. This is a concept note to react to, not a fixed plan.

Overview

Lift up the people doing the work

Skoll finds and backs the social entrepreneurs solving the world's hardest problems. Gallup listens to the world and turns what it hears into evidence leaders act on. Together we could give Skoll's community something it rarely has: credible, comparable data about the problems its innovators are solving, built to travel through the Forum and the press.

For more than twenty years, the Skoll Foundation has bet on good people doing good things, giving them multi-year, unrestricted backing and then championing their work through storytelling, convening and thought leadership. The Skoll World Forum in Oxford is where that community comes together each spring, alongside a far larger social impact week that draws thousands more people to the city.

This is a demanding moment for that community. A sharp pullback in global development funding has left many organizations rethinking how they sustain their work. In that environment, independent evidence does real work: it shows funders why a problem matters, gives awardees numbers to build their campaigns around, and helps a fragmented field see what it has in common. A Gallup partnership would not be about attention for Skoll. It would put better evidence in the hands of the awardees and the wider community, so their work carries further.

What a partnership could do

Gallup can reach people in the places Skoll's awardees work, including communities where little reliable data exists, and ask the questions that matter for their issues. That evidence can anchor the announcements at the Forum, carry the storytelling through the year, and, if useful, become an ongoing measure the community returns to rather than a single release tied to one moment.

The principle behind every option here

The research is for the awardees and the community, not for Skoll. Every option in this note is designed to make the innovators' work more visible, better understood, and easier to fund.

About Gallup

A global analytics and advisory firm

Founded in 1935 and headquartered in Washington, D.C., Gallup studies what people around the world think, need and experience, and turns that into evidence leaders use. Its public findings regularly become reference points cited by newsrooms, policymakers and organizations.

The engine: the Gallup World Poll

Since 2005, the Gallup World Poll has surveyed people in more than 140 countries every year, using nationally representative, probability-based samples that reach roughly 98% of the world's adults. In most lower and middle income countries, interviews happen face to face, reaching rural and underserved communities that other surveys miss.

For a foundation, that reach means a question can be asked of the exact populations its grantees serve, across Africa, South Asia, Latin America and beyond, and answered to a standard the public and the press trust. Because the platform runs every year, a question added now can become a measure the community tracks over time.

Past Work

Three studies Gallup has run with other funders

Each of these started the way Skoll's could: a mission-driven partner, a question the world had never measured well, and a decision to do it rigorously and release it openly. Each is powered by the Gallup World Poll, and each became a reference point in its field.

Health · Wellcome Trust

Wellcome Global Monitor

The largest study ever conducted of how the world thinks and feels about science and health, built to help funders understand public trust.

ScaleMore than 140,000 people across over 140 countries, in some 140 languages.
What it measuredTrust in scientists, doctors and vaccines, and who feels included in the benefits of science.
Became the global benchmark on public trust in science, widely cited by researchers, funders and the press.
Safety · Lloyd's Register Foundation

World Risk Poll

The first global study of what people worry about and the harm they actually experience, built to fill data gaps in places with little official information.

ScaleAround 150,000 people in more than 140 countries, representing 98% of the world's population.
What it measuredPerceived risk and lived experience of harm, from workplaces to climate to online safety.
A repeating evidence base, co-funded in modules by partners, now used by UN bodies and governments.
Economic opportunity · World Bank

Global Findex

The world's most comprehensive measure of how adults save, borrow, pay and manage risk, making financial inclusion measurable for the first time.

ScaleMore than 150,000 adults across 140 plus economies, updated every three years since 2011.
What it measuredAccount ownership and financial access, including for women, the poor and rural residents.
Set the global standard for financial inclusion and anchored a worldwide goal to reach the unbanked.

Each study is conducted by Gallup as part of, or alongside, the Gallup World Poll. Scale figures are drawn from the partners' own published materials.

01 Evidence for Skoll's priority areas Rigorous data on the issues at the heart of Skoll's grantmaking: climate, community health and economic opportunity. Program
areas

Skoll has sharpened its focus to three areas: climate, through preventing deforestation; global public health, through community health workers; and economic opportunity, through smallholder agriculture and girls' education. This option builds research directly on those areas, producing evidence that strengthens every awardee working in them and gives Skoll's issue leads a shared fact base. Pick an area below to see the kinds of studies it could support.

Studies within each priority area
Focus: preventing deforestation
  • How communities near forests depend on them for income, food and fuel, and what they stand to lose.
  • Whether people see deforestation as a threat to their own future and their children's.
  • Trust in who should protect forests: local groups, governments or outside organizations.
  • Awareness and support for the kinds of solutions awardees are building on the ground.
Focus: community health workers
  • How far people must travel to reach trained, trusted health care, and what stops them.
  • The role community health workers already play, and public trust in them.
  • Gaps in maternal, child and preventive care that community-based models can close.
  • Where investment in frontline health workers would have the most effect.
Focus: smallholder agriculture, Global South
  • Barriers smallholder farmers face: access to credit, markets, inputs and information.
  • How climate pressure is changing what farmers can grow and earn.
  • Which support actually reaches farmers, and which passes them by.
  • The resilience and income effects of the approaches awardees are scaling.
Focus: girls' education
  • Where girls fall out of the education system, and the reasons families give.
  • Community attitudes toward keeping girls in school through adolescence.
  • The link between a girl finishing school and later work, income and family outcomes.
  • What families say would help most, as evidence for program design and funding.

04 The drive to solve local problems A global read on who has the energy to fix things in their own community, where that energy is strongest, and what stands in the way. The people
behind change

Skoll's whole model rests on a belief that there are good people everywhere ready to solve the problems around them. This option measures that directly, across the world. It does not ask people whether they are social entrepreneurs, a term most would not recognize. Instead it backs into the idea through plain questions people do understand, about wanting to improve their community, having an idea, and what holds them back. The result maps where the drive to build change already exists and what it would take to unlock it.

Impact
For fundersEvidence that investing in local changemakers is investing in a real, measurable population.
For awardeesA picture of the latent demand and talent in the markets where they recruit and work.
For the narrativeA fresh, hopeful story: change is not scarce, it is waiting on support.
